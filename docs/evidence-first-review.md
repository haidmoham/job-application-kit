# Evidence-first role and resume review

This is a reusable review method, not a candidate profile, numeric score, or
second application tracker. Keep candidate facts and source records private.

## Separate four questions

1. Is the role within the authorized search scope and hard constraints?
2. What paid, project, adjacent, or unknown evidence supports each material duty?
3. Does the selected resume place the relevant evidence where a reviewer can find it?
4. What outcome was observed, and how long has the opportunity been followed?

Keyword routing answers none of these questions completely. A route identifies
a document or lane; it does not establish candidate qualification.

## Review requirements without hiding gaps

Read the complete duties and qualifications. Map each material requirement to
a specific source and preserve requirements that are not met. Distinguish
required experience, preferred tools, and explicitly accepted equivalents.
Do not convert a preference into a hard gate. Do not infer exact years of a
technology from total employment duration.

Paid experience, independent projects, and adjacent responsibilities are distinct
forms of evidence. A project may demonstrate a skill without establishing prior
professional ownership. Internal stakeholder work is not automatically prospect
work; a technical prototype is not automatically a sales evaluation. Technical
communication does not prove a commercial track record. The candidate's formal
title is evidence, not a complete measure of ability or a reason to invent a title.

A strong-match label needs support for the actual core work and responsibility
level. Record material transitions rather than hiding them inside an overall
impression. A stretch may be deliberate and worth attempting; it remains a stretch.
Do not fill a target lane percentage or a strong/stretch quota by relabeling roles.

## Resume readiness

Evaluate each resume against the work it is meant to support. For experienced
candidates, relevant paid work normally precedes unrelated independent projects.
Use a specialist project first only when that choice serves the actual role.
Preserve titles, dates, attribution, and bounded claims. A relevant work sample
should reduce uncertainty, not require the reviewer to translate an unrelated project.

Onboarding assesses and reports; it does not silently rewrite a resume. Follow the
repository's existing explicit-authorization rule for creation or edits. Preserve
originals, use an immutable revised artifact, verify rendering and reading order,
and record the exact version used. Do not fall back to a stale file without disclosure.
Keep confidence and its reason in every readiness assessment.

## Outcome interpretation

Use the existing local application history. Record application, review, recruiter
screen, technical interview, final round, and offer separately. Missing replies
remain unresolved; recent cohorts are right-censored. Separate role family,
artifact version, fit, source channel, and observation window before comparing rates.
Do not infer specific rejection causes from boilerplate, or infer that ability is
either deficient or sufficient merely from a lack of interviews.

A before/after change in both targeting and packaging is a corrective bundle,
not a controlled estimate of either intervention. Record predictions and observations
separately. General studies may motivate a test but do not provide a candidate's
personal conversion rate. Application volume is an execution count, not a career outcome.

## Privacy and scope

Do not commit candidate names, resumes, email evidence, private document links,
financial circumstances, application history, or local configuration into this kit.
No application, outreach, or account change is authorized by this review method.
Preserve existing security, ethics, deduplication, and confirmation requirements.
